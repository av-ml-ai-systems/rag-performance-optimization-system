# Northstar Technologies — IT Security Policy

**Document ID:** IT-POL-005  
**Department:** Information Technology and Security  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the minimum information security requirements for protecting Northstar Technologies' systems, devices, networks, applications, and data.

It applies to employees, contractors, and other authorized individuals who access company information or technology resources.

The objectives are to prevent unauthorized access, reduce security risks, protect confidential information, and support the availability and integrity of business systems.

## 2. Access Control

Access to company systems and information must be authorized and limited to what an individual needs to perform their responsibilities.

Employees must use their own assigned accounts and must not share passwords, authentication codes, access tokens, or other credentials.

Access requests must follow the approved IT access process. Managers are responsible for confirming the business need for access when approval is required.

Access privileges must be reviewed when employees change roles or responsibilities. Access must be revoked promptly when an individual no longer requires it, including when employment or a contract ends.

Employees must not attempt to access systems, files, or accounts for which they have not been authorized.

## 3. Passwords and Authentication

Employees must use strong, unique passwords for company accounts and must not reuse company passwords on personal websites or services.

Multi-factor authentication must be enabled for systems that support or require it under company security standards.

Passwords and authentication codes must not be shared through email, chat, documents, or other communication channels.

Employees must use an approved password manager when one is provided or required by IT.

If an employee suspects that a password or authentication factor has been compromised, the employee must report the incident to IT immediately and follow instructions to secure the account.

## 4. Company Devices

Company-managed laptops, mobile devices, and other equipment must be used responsibly and protected against loss, theft, and unauthorized access.

Employees must:

- Lock their screens whenever leaving devices unattended.
- Use approved operating systems, applications, and security tools.
- Install required security updates promptly.
- Avoid disabling endpoint protection, firewalls, or other security controls.
- Keep devices physically secure during travel and remote work.
- Report lost, stolen, or potentially compromised devices immediately.

Employees must not install unauthorized software or make changes that weaken device security.

Personal devices may access company resources only when permitted by the applicable bring-your-own-device requirements and approved security controls.

## 5. Data Classification and Handling

Company information must be handled according to its sensitivity and business purpose.

Employees must protect confidential information from unauthorized disclosure, alteration, copying, or loss.

Sensitive information must be stored only in approved systems and shared only with authorized recipients.

Employees must verify recipient identities and permissions before sharing confidential files or granting access to documents.

Confidential information must not be transferred to personal email accounts, unapproved file-sharing services, or unauthorized storage devices.

Information that is no longer required must be retained or disposed of according to applicable retention and data-handling requirements.

## 6. Email and Phishing

Employees must exercise caution when opening email attachments, clicking links, scanning unexpected QR codes, or responding to unusual requests.

Warning signs may include:

- Unexpected requests for credentials or authentication codes.
- Messages creating unusual urgency or pressure.
- Unexpected attachments or links.
- Sender addresses that do not match the claimed organization.
- Requests to transfer money or disclose confidential information.
- Messages that bypass normal approval procedures.

Employees must report suspected phishing or other suspicious communications through the designated security reporting channel.

They must not forward suspicious messages to colleagues as a warning unless instructed to do so through an approved reporting procedure.

If an employee clicks a suspicious link, opens a potentially malicious attachment, or enters credentials into a questionable website, the employee must report the event immediately, even if no obvious damage has occurred.

## 7. Network and Remote Access

Employees must use approved methods when connecting remotely to company systems.

Where required, remote access must use company-approved virtual private network (VPN) services or other authorized secure access mechanisms.

Employees should avoid conducting confidential business over unsecured public Wi-Fi networks. If public connectivity is unavoidable, they must use the security measures required by IT.

Employees must not bypass network security controls or connect unauthorized devices to company networks.

Home networks used for company work should be protected by a strong router administrator password and current security settings.

## 8. Software Installation and Updates

Only authorized software may be installed on company-managed devices.

Employees must obtain approval from IT before installing software that requires elevated privileges, introduces new security risks, or accesses sensitive company information.

Security updates must be installed within the time frames established by IT.

Employees must not disable automatic updates or endpoint protection to improve performance or avoid interruptions without explicit authorization.

Software vulnerabilities discovered during normal work must be reported to IT through the approved support or security process.

## 9. Cloud Services and External Tools

Employees must use company-approved cloud services for storing, processing, and sharing business information.

Before introducing a new external service, employees must follow the applicable security, privacy, procurement, and approval requirements.

Confidential company information must not be uploaded to unapproved external tools.

Employees must review sharing permissions and access settings before publishing or distributing information through cloud platforms.

Third-party integrations must be authorized and granted only the permissions required for their approved business purpose.

## 10. Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence

Employees may use AI tools for approved business purposes, subject to company data protection and security requirements.

Confidential information, personal data, credentials, source code, customer records, and other restricted information must not be entered into public or unapproved AI tools.

Employees must use company-approved AI services when processing information that requires controlled access or additional privacy safeguards.

AI-generated content must be reviewed for accuracy, relevance, and potential disclosure of sensitive information before it is used in business decisions or shared externally.

AI tools must not be granted access to company systems, files, or accounts without the appropriate authorization.

Employees should consult IT or their manager when they are uncertain whether an AI tool is approved for a particular use case.

## 11. Security Incident Reporting

Employees must report suspected or confirmed security incidents immediately through the designated IT or security reporting channel.

Examples include:

- Lost or stolen company devices.
- Suspected compromised accounts.
- Accidental disclosure of confidential information.
- Malware or ransomware alerts.
- Unauthorized access to systems or files.
- Suspicious network activity.
- Data sent to an unintended recipient.
- Exposure of credentials or access tokens.

Employees should provide the time of discovery, affected systems, relevant observations, and actions already taken when this information is available.

Employees must not attempt to investigate, erase evidence, or independently remediate a significant incident unless instructed by the authorized response team.

Prompt reporting is required even when the employee is uncertain whether a security incident has actually occurred.

## 12. Backup and Data Recovery

Business-critical information must be stored in approved locations covered by the company's backup and recovery arrangements.

Employees must not rely exclusively on local device storage for important business documents.

IT is responsible for maintaining applicable backup processes and coordinating recovery procedures for supported systems.

Employees must report suspected data loss or accidental deletion promptly to improve the likelihood of successful recovery.

Backup copies must be protected against unauthorized access and handled according to the applicable retention and security requirements.

## 13. Physical Security

Employees must prevent unauthorized individuals from accessing company facilities, equipment, and sensitive documents.

Visitors must follow the company's visitor procedures and must not be granted access to restricted areas without authorization.

Employees should secure printed confidential information when it is not in use and dispose of it through approved secure disposal methods.

Company devices must not be left unattended in vehicles or other locations where theft or unauthorized access is likely.

Security badges, keys, and other physical access credentials must not be shared.

## 14. Responsibilities

**Employees and contractors** must follow security requirements, protect their credentials and devices, handle data appropriately, and report suspected incidents promptly.

**Managers** must ensure that team members understand relevant security responsibilities and that access requests reflect legitimate business needs.

**Information Technology and Security** is responsible for maintaining security controls, managing access processes, communicating requirements, and coordinating incident response.

**Human Resources** must coordinate relevant access changes associated with employee onboarding, role changes, leave arrangements where applicable, and offboarding.

All individuals with access to company systems share responsibility for protecting company information.

## 15. Policy Violations

Violations of this policy may result in access restrictions, mandatory security training, corrective action, or disciplinary measures in accordance with company procedures and applicable law.

Suspected criminal activity, deliberate unauthorized access, or intentional data theft may be escalated to the appropriate internal or external authorities.

Employees are encouraged to report security concerns in good faith. Reports should be handled through appropriate channels and reviewed according to established procedures.

## 16. Review and Updates

Information Technology and Security is responsible for reviewing this policy periodically and updating it as threats, technologies, business requirements, and applicable legal obligations change.

Employees must follow the current approved version and comply with additional security procedures communicated by IT.
